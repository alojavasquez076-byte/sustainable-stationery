Target Items: Pens & Recycling Materials

Understanding what goes into specialty drop-off boxes versus standard curbside recycling bins.

🖊️

Pens & Writing Instruments

  • Ballpoint & Gel Pens: Mixed plastics and metal springs cannot go in regular bins—collect them for TerraCycle or school pen drives.
  • Markers & Highlighters: Dried markers can be repurposed or sent to specialty stationery recycling programs.
  • Refillable Pens: Keep the outer barrel and replace only the ink cartridge to cut plastic waste by 80%.
📦

Paper & Cardboard

  • Notebooks & Office Paper: Clean, dry sheets are 100% recyclable. Remove plastic spiral bindings first.
  • Cardboard Boxes: Flatten boxes to save space in community recycling bins.
  • Contamination Rule: Keep paper away from food grease or spilled ink, which ruins paper pulp.
🧴

Plastics, Cans & Bottles

  • PET Bottles (#1) & HDPE (#2): Empty, rinse, and crush plastic bottles before tossing them in the bin.
  • Aluminum & Tin Cans: Infinitely recyclable—recycling one aluminum can saves enough energy to run a TV for 3 hours.
  • Keep Caps On: Leave plastic caps screwed onto empty bottles so small caps don't get lost in sorting machines.

Significance to Our Community

Every year, billions of disposable plastic pens and mis-sorted recyclables end up in local landfills and waterways because people are unsure which bin to use.

By bringing accessible AI classification into classrooms, barangay centers, and offices, this tool empowers residents to sort waste accurately at the source—reducing recycling contamination, lowering municipal waste costs, and building a cleaner, greener neighborhood for everyone.
